Approach

How I design a learning program

The method stays the same from a ministry to a community hall. What changes is everything on the surface: the cases, the language, the pairings, the pace.

Start from the people

Who is in the room, what their context allows, and what they need to be able to do afterwards.

Indicators before content

Learning indicators are written first, so there is something to measure at the end.

Experiential and participatory

Exercises on the participants' own problems, with interest-based framing and trauma-informed principles where the topic needs them.

Write it down for others

Facilitator guides, lesson plans, workbooks, case studies and toolkits, so quality does not depend on one facilitator.

Prepare the facilitators

Onboarding, briefings, common standards and feedback mechanisms for co-facilitators and breakout leaders.

Debrief and refine

Post-session debriefs and evaluations, and a course that changes from what the learners said and did.

Experience

Two strands, one career

Designing and facilitating learning on one side; on the other, the program management that makes it happen: donor compliance, partners, budgets, field teams, closeouts.
